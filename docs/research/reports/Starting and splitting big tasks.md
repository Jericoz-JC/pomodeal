# Start big tasks before planning them

The research points one way: students get going on a 2 to 4 hour, multi-day task when the first step is small, concrete and named, and planning comes after they have started. A detailed plan made up front does not do this. The best-supported starting tool is the if-then plan ("when I sit down, I'll first..."). Its pooled effect has shrunk from d = 0.65 to about d = 0.15 to 0.35 after newer bias corrections, but it is still the most reliable lever available. Unpacking a task into pieces corrects optimistic time estimates. Stopping with a written next step helps people resume, through the Ovsiankina resumption tendency and not the Zeigarnik memory effect, which failed to replicate. The product landscape shows the gap Pomodeal can fill. Starter tools like Goblin Tools forget you once the tab closes. Planners like Motion, Marvin and Tiimo lose people to setup cost, guilt surfaces and re-planning nobody asked for. Nobody designs for the day after a missed session. The council of three seats converged on one four-week phase. A big task gets a 15-minute "down payment" block with a named first move before any planning screen. Each block ends with a one-line bookmark. Unfinished time is kept as "open terms", never "overdue", so day 2 always opens on a one-tap card, and a missed day leads to a comeback screen that offers a smaller first move. Web Push (block boundaries only), install onboarding, the privacy inventory, task-scoped history, chosen repayment and a weekly one-question check-in come into the phase. An optional Haiku 5.5 "suggest pieces" button arrives in week 4 and is the first thing cut. The rest of the roadmap waits, and nothing is deleted.

## A named first move beats a plan, and the evidence says how hard to lean on it

Procrastination is best understood as **short-term mood repair**: people avoid the bad feeling a task produces now, and their future self pays for it ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12011)). Steel's meta-analysis of 691 correlations found task aversiveness, delay, low self-efficacy and impulsiveness to be the strongest correlates. It estimated that **80 to 95% of college students procrastinate**, about half of them consistently ([Steel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.1.65)). Intentions alone do little: a medium-to-large change in intention (d = 0.66) moved behaviour only d = 0.36 ([Webb & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249)). The design consequence is to lower the emotional cost of the first minute and not add pressure. That is why the phase puts a short, concrete block ahead of any planning screen.

The strongest single lever is the **implementation intention**. A 2006 meta-analysis of 94 tests found d = 0.65 on goal attainment, and getting started was one of the problems it addressed best ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, NCI summary](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/constructs/implementation_intentions/goal_intent_attain.pdf)). The honest number is now lower. A 2025 update across **642 tests** reports d = 0.35 after trim-and-fill and **d = 0.15 (95% CI 0.08 to 0.22)** with robust Bayesian correction, with the uncorrected average at d = 0.36, all checked against the paper's full text ([Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2025, European Review of Social Psychology 36(1), 162-194](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563); open-access copy at the [Konstanz repository](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)). The same paper says how to write the plan. If-then plans beat schedule-style plans (d = .43 vs .29), naming a time and place helps (d = .46), and adding how or for how long to that cue seemed to hurt (d = .24). Plans work best when the goal is wanted (d = .79 for strong, self-concordant intentions), and the effect on behaviour specifically is d = .27. So the first move should be one short if-then line, not a detailed script. Procrastination interventions in general show a small effect against inactive controls, g = 0.34 ([Rozental et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01588/pdf)). Concrete thinking supports the same move. Students who thought about *how* to do a task finished it sooner than those who thought about *why* ([McCrea et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02240.x)). Students who rehearsed the process of studying started earlier and scored higher than those who imagined the grade ([Pham & Taylor 1999](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167299025002010)). A hand-typed first move on the deal ticket captures all of this at almost no cost in UI or compute. An AI-written ten-step plan the user did not author is not an implementation intention, and it may weaken ownership.

Splitting has real but narrower support. Unpacking a multi-step task makes people give longer, more realistic time estimates ([Kruger & Evans 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2003.11.001)). Time allocated to subtasks sums to more than time allocated to the whole ([Forsyth & Burt 2008](https://dx.doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.4.791)), though those tasks lasted minutes, not hours. Students in the classic study predicted about 34 days for their theses and took about 56 ([Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366)). Nothing in this literature says the unpacking must happen before the first block. That is why the council moved the gut-versus-pieces comparison to day 2, when the task already has some pull. That pull is the **Ovsiankina effect**. A 2025 meta-analysis found a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks, but **no memory advantage for unfinished tasks**, so the Zeigarnik claim does not hold ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). The bookmark rests on resumption plus a concrete next action, not on "your brain keeps thinking about it".

For progress display, people are more motivated when attention goes to whichever part is smaller: done early on, remaining later ([Koo & Fishbach 2012](https://doi.org/10.1086/663827)). That gives the "2 stamped" then "2 to go" switch at halfway. For the comeback screen, students induced to be self-compassionate after failing a test studied *longer* for the retest ([Breines & Chen 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212445599)). The self-forgiveness study often cited for procrastination is a single correlational one ([BPS Research Digest on Wohl et al. 2010](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/cure-procrastination-forgive-yourself)). The fresh-start effect is archival and has a documented backfire mode ([Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901); [Dai dissertation](https://repository.upenn.edu/edissertations/1035/)), so the phase does not offer "start Monday" re-plans.

Exam studying has the firmest ground of the four task types. Distributed practice beats massed practice across 317 experiments ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)). Practice testing and distributed practice are the two "high utility" techniques, while rereading and highlighting rate low ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)). Retrieval beats restudy at about g = 0.50 to 0.61 ([Rowland 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559); [Adesope et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306)). This is why exam study defaults to two or more days. Even spreading for the other three types has weaker support: the classic self-imposed-deadlines study (Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002) was [retracted in September 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261488042), so for writing, problem sets and projects the spread is a suggestion resting on thin evidence. Writing rests on older faculty data showing that brief regular sessions out-produce deadline binges ([Boice 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-7967(89)90144-7)). Open-ended projects suit specific *learning* goals over performance goals ([Locke & Latham 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705)).

On timer structure, neither of two small student RCTs found a difference in task completion between break schemes. Smits et al. 2025 found no overall differences between Pomodoro, self-regulated and Flowtime breaks (completion 70%, 69% and 67%, p = .854, 94 students) ([Smits et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/)). Biwer et al. 2023 found that self-regulated breaks came with more fatigue and distraction and lower concentration and motivation than systematic breaks ([Biwer et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/)). That is the main reason earned breaks wait: break schemes are not a starting lever.

| Strength | What it supports in this phase | Key sources |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | If-then first move (smaller effect than once thought); spacing and retrieval for exam study | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 and the 2025 update; Cepeda 2006; Dunlosky 2013 |
| Moderate | Concrete first step; unpacking and segmentation (short lab tasks); resumption after interruption; small-area progress; self-compassion after a lapse | McCrea 2008; Kruger & Evans 2004; Ghibellini & Meier 2025; Koo & Fishbach 2012; Breines & Chen 2012 |
| Thin | Any specific minute count for "just start"; self-forgiveness; body doubling; segmentation for multi-day student work; AI breakdown improving completion; brief regular writing sessions | No controlled trial found; Wohl 2010 single study; a 12-person VR study and a null controlled study; Boice 1989 (older, faculty sample, no student study found) |

The 15-minute down payment is a default the evidence points toward, not a number it proves. No controlled trial of "tiny first step" or "2-minute rule" interventions was found, and those claims trace to popular books. Every DOI in this report was checked against Crossref on 2026-11-08 and resolves to the cited paper, and the implementation-intention figures were checked against the paper's full text. For the other classic papers only the citation is confirmed, not the quoted figures, so spot-check a figure before it goes into public copy.

### Claims to keep out of copy

| Do not say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Your willpower is running low" / "recharge" | Ego depletion failed registered replications: d ≈ 0.04 across 23 labs ([Hagger et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873)) and d ≈ 0.06 across 36 labs ([Vohs et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797621989733)) | "The task got more expensive than the alternatives" (the opportunity-cost model, [Kurzban et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12003196)) |
| "Unfinished tasks stay on your mind" | Zeigarnik memory advantage not supported ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)) | "Stopping with a next step makes restarting easier" |
| "21 days to a habit" | Median about 66 days with a range of roughly 18 to 254 ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)); also conflicts with the no-streaks rule | Nothing; don't frame habits at all |
| "25 minutes is the optimal focus length" | No evidence it beats other schemes ([Smits et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/)) | Present lengths as choices |
| "Research shows 15 minutes is the magic number" | No trial of a specific starting length exists | "A short first block is easier to begin" |
| "Breaks boost performance" | Micro-breaks help fatigue (d ≈ .35) but not performance on demanding work ([Albulescu et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/c6f37b6ba159469296b2cb968b65505f)) | Breaks as rest you planned, not fuel |
| Learning styles, 90-minute ultradian cycles, 8-second attention spans | Unsupported ([Pashler et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) on learning styles; no peer-reviewed support found for the others) | Nothing |
| Anything clinical or diagnostic | Product rule: never an ADHD or health product | Plain behaviour words |

## Competitors either forget the task or take over the plan

The market splits into **starter toys** that are instant and stateless, and **planner systems** that are stateful and heavy. Neither handles the day after. Goblin Tools' Magic ToDo breaks tasks down with a "spiciness" slider that asks how hard the task *feels*, which review sentiment suggests is the most praised control in the category (our inference; see the [Warwick library guide](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/using/productivity-tools/magic-todo-gobin-tools)). Its complaints are the mirror image of the planners'. Breakdowns can be excessive ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/goblin-tools)), it does no scheduling, and without an account a breakdown is gone once the tab closes ([Thawly review](https://thawly.ai/reviews/goblin-tools)). Sunsama users have even asked for a Goblin integration inside their planner ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/integrations/p/goblin-tools)).

The planners lose people in other ways. Amazing Marvin offers 100+ strategies, including a Procrastination Wizard and a still "Coming Soon" Smallest Step feature, and it shows a reschedule counter as exclamation marks while saying the counter is "not to shame you" ([Marvin for procrastinators](https://amazingmarvin.com/for/procrastinators/)). Reviewers call its first week overwhelming ([Saner.ai review, a competitor](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/amazing-marvin-review)). Motion's most cited complaint is that its AI reshuffles the whole day without a suggest-then-approve mode ([Morgen review, a competitor](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-review); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/214264/motion)). Tiimo was Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiimo)). One unverified competitor analysis attributes about 27% of its recent low ratings to AI-focused changes ([Soothfy](https://soothfy.com/blog/tiimo-alternatives.html)). Sunsama lets the user *choose* what rolls over (automatically, or a prompt to pick) ([Sunsama help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/evening-daily-planning)). That choice is the model for Pomodeal's "chosen" repayment.

The most useful external evidence comes from one sec, a friction app that pauses before distracting apps. A PNAS field study found that it cut opening of target apps by 57%, and in a follow-up experiment **the option to dismiss** had the strongest effect (the time delay also helped), while a deliberation message alone did not ([one sec / Max Planck study](https://one-sec.app/max-planck-study)). Autonomy carries the effect. That supports Pomodeal's "I need this always works" rule, the exam-spacing default the user can override, and the suggest-then-sign rule for any re-spread.

Positioning follows. Pomodeal can be as light as Goblin at the start and still **remember where you left off** across days, which none of the starter tools do. It can also refuse the planners' failure modes by never planning before the first block and never reshuffling without a signature. "First step, no streaks" alone is getting crowded: a 2026 App Store entrant markets one small first step and "calm daily rhythm instead of streaks or guilt" ([Ravi on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755695867)). What is still unclaimed is the **re-entry** moment (bookmark, open terms, comeback screen) and the **negotiation** frame, where the user sets the terms of each block. No product found designs explicitly for a missed day. Two caveats: nearly every ranked review in this space is written by a competing vendor, and the research did not reach primary Reddit threads, so community sentiment here is second-hand.

## The stack carries the phase if push stays a courtesy

Every reminder in this phase depends on one fragile step: **Home Screen install on iOS**. Web Push works only for installed web apps on iOS 16.4+ ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). **Declarative Web Push** in iOS 18.4 removes the service-worker requirement and the silent-push penalties, and its JSON payload carries a `navigate` deep link and an `app_badge` value ([WebKit: Meet Declarative Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)). The declarative shape degrades to a service-worker handler on older iOS, so one payload format serves both. Screen Wake Lock was broken in installed iOS apps until 18.4 ([WebKit bug 254545](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254545)). Since Safari 26, any site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default ([WebKit: Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). iOS still has no background sync, no widgets and no manifest shortcuts ([TapPWA](https://tappwa.com/blog/ios-pwa-limitations-2026)). One author estimates iOS install rates at **30 to 50% of Android's**, an anecdotal figure. On Android, FCM can defer even high-priority messages to a Doze maintenance window ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/customize-messages/setting-message-priority)). Sources conflict on whether iOS shows notification action buttons, so plan for none. The conclusion is firm: **block boundaries stay client-side** from `startedAt + duration` with a server-corrected clock, and push only adds to that. Day 2 has to work when the user simply opens the app.

Convex fits the boundary model. `runAt` scheduled functions are durable across restarts. Mutations run exactly once, but **actions run at most once and are never retried** ([Convex scheduled functions](https://docs.convex.dev/scheduling/scheduled-functions)). Every push send therefore goes mutation, then scheduled internal action, then result mutation, with a `{blockId, version}` check that turns a stale or double-fired boundary into a no-op. The free tier allows **1,000,000 function calls a month** but only **8 concurrent scheduled jobs**, against 256 on Professional ([Convex limits](https://docs.convex.dev/production/state/limits)). At 100 users, 6 blocks a day and 2 boundaries per block, that is about 1,200 scheduled runs a day, roughly 36,000 a month: **under 4% of the free call budget**. The concurrency cap is the one that bites. A synchronized "everyone starts at 7" room feature would queue sends for seconds, which is one more reason rooms wait. Dropping the daily "next due" sweep keeps push tied to boundaries, adds no standing reminder channel, and leaves no per-plan scheduling to debug.

The optional AI is cheap enough that cost is not the argument. **Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens** ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). With a 1,500-token system prompt, a 300-token task description and a 500-token JSON reply, one suggestion costs about **$0.00043**. Five a day is about $0.065 per user per month, and the whole beta at a realistic half a suggestion per user per day costs under a dollar a month. Sonnet 5.5 ($2 / $10) would be about $0.0086 a call. With a global cap of 300 calls a day, the worst case is $2.58 a day on Sonnet and about $0.13 a day on Haiku. Structured outputs are generally available and constrain the reply to a JSON schema. The schema cannot express `maxItems` or numeric bounds, so the six-piece cap and the minute ranges must be enforced in code ([Structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). `@convex-dev/rate-limiter` gives a per-user token bucket checked inside the mutation, so an over-limit request never reaches the API ([README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@convex-dev/rate-limiter@0.3.2/README.md)). The real costs are elsewhere. On privacy, API inputs are kept up to **30 days by default**, zero retention is by sales arrangement, and caching or structured outputs can override it ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). On product, a 2 to 5 second wait in front of Start would add friction to the one thing Pomodeal does well, and no RCT shows that AI breakdowns improve completion.

The iOS Shortcuts Intercept stays a recipe because Apple makes it one. Run Immediately automations must always post a banner ([Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/automations-run-immediately-shortcuts-notifications)). Single forum anecdotes describe prompting returning in iOS 18.2 ([MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/automation-set-to-run-immediately-keeps-asking-for-confirmation-since-18-2.2445766)) and a test in which **6 of 18** runs succeeded ([MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/automation-control-shortcuts-failing.2430032); see also [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255820198)). The loop fix one sec alternatives use is a native iOS 26 App Intents API that a PWA cannot reach ([DEV](https://dev.to/eliguzz/building-an-open-source-one-sec-alternative-breaking-the-shortcuts-infinite-loop-thanks-to-ios-26-ka4)). Offline behaviour needs one guard. The Convex client holds unsent mutations in memory, not on disk ([Convex Discord](https://discord-questions.convex.dev/m/1242127900052819998)), so a deal made in a lecture-hall dead zone can be lost if iOS kills the app. Deals need a client-generated id, server-side dedupe and a visible "saving" state.

Last, a beta of friends cannot measure retention. Productivity apps keep about **9 to 10% of users at day 30**, the best of any category ([Plotline](https://www.plotline.so/blog/retention-rates-mobile-apps-by-industry)). Gamification effects start fading after about four weeks ([Rodrigues et al.](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1203232)). The phase therefore measures behaviour around starting, not retention.

## The merged phase: a down payment, a bookmark, and open terms

The phase has one idea: start before planning. Everything else either makes the next start cheaper or keeps a missed day from turning into guilt. "Deal" stays the word for one block. A multi-block task is a **big one** in the UI and lives under the user's own title ("Paper"). Its parts are **pieces**, unfinished time is **open terms**, and the table is `tasks`. "Contract" never appears in user-facing copy, because it turns a missed day into a breach.

### Day 1: name it, take two optional taps, start

The user types "Paper due Friday" into Quick Deal. A chip offers **"Bigger than one block?"** Tapping it shows two optional, skippable tap rows: task type (Writing / Problems & code / Exam study / Project) and gut size (1h / 2h / 3h / 4h+). Then comes the ticket: "First payment on Paper: 15 min. First move: open the doc and write one ugly sentence. Deal?" The first move is prefilled by type and editable, and 30 minutes is one tap away. The 15-minute length is new and exists only for a big task's first block, as one pure rule in `packages/core`. The user plans the break as usual, stamps the ticket, and the block runs. No split, plan, due-date or segment screen appears before this block. When the block ends, the stamp screen shows at most two prompts. The first is the **bookmark**: "Next time, start with...", one skippable line. The second is **done**, using the type's done criterion, for example "Messy draft of the intro exists?". Whatever the user does, the remaining time (gut size minus time worked) is saved as open terms, so day 2 always has a card.

### Day 2: the card leads, and planning becomes optional

Opening the app, Quick Deal leads with the task card: "Paper: start with 'claim for paragraph 2'. 30 min?" One tap starts a block whose first move is the bookmark. Progress follows the small-area rule ("2 stamped" before halfway, "2 to go" after). This card is where planning lives, all of it optional. **Split into pieces** shows type-based slots and the gut-versus-pieces comparison ("your pieces add up to 3h 40; your gut said 2h", shown without comment). **Spread over the days left** asks for the due date if it is missing and proposes evenly spaced pieces, which the user signs or edits. Exam study defaults to two or more days with a one-line why, for example "Two shorter days usually stick better than one long night. Change it if you need to." In week 4, an optional **Suggest pieces** button fills the slots for editing. If installed, the 2-minute heads-up and block-end pushes fire for any running block. No push ever says "time to work on Paper".

### Missed day: a smaller move, never a ledger of misses

When the user returns after a gap, the first screen never shows what was missed. There are no counts, no red, and nothing is ever "overdue". It offers one thing: "Paper's still here. Smaller first move today?", with a shrunk first move. The user can also clear open terms in one tap ("Start fresh on this one?"). If a spread had been signed, a re-spread of the remaining pieces is **suggested after** that block, and the user signs it or haggles. Nothing reshuffles silently. No "fresh start Monday" offer appears, because that pattern has a documented backfire mode.

### Four task types run on one engine

The types differ only in placeholder strings, units and defaults. The engine is the same.

| Type | Default first move | Piece unit | Done stamp | Typical bookmark | Spread default | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | "Open the doc, write one ugly sentence" | Section | Messy draft exists; revising is its own piece | "Claim for paragraph 2" | Even across days left | Thin to moderate (Boice thin; concrete construal moderate) |
| Problems / code | "Problem 1 until stuck" / "Run it and read the first error" | N problems or one feature | Attempted, and the stuck question is written down | The stuck question | Even across days left | Moderate; no coding-specific study found |
| Exam study | "Self-quiz topic 1 without notes" | Topic | Recalled each item once without notes | "Weak spot: enzyme kinetics" | Two or more separate days, overridable with a one-line why | Strong (spacing, retrieval) |
| Project | "Decide one thing: ___" | Decision or artifact | "Decided X" / "sketch of Y exists" | "Next visible thing: outline" | Even across days left | Moderate (learning goals for complex work) |

Exam progress is counted in topics practiced, never in hours, because counting hours rewards passive rereading.

```ts
// convex/schema.ts (sketch; every field listed in docs/specs/006-privacy-inventory.md)
tasks: defineTable({

  title: v.string(),                    // user's own words: "Paper"

// sessions (the block table) gains: taskId?, pieceIndex?, firstMove?, dealId (client-generated), version, scheduledIds[]
```

Task-scoped history is a query over `sessions` by `taskId`, not a new feature. Time is still computed from stored `startedAt` plus duration.

### How the beta will know it worked

With 8 to 15 friends, these numbers are descriptive and not significance tests. The four targets are: **at least 70%** of big tasks have their first block running within 2 minutes of naming, without AI; **at least 60%** of multi-day big tasks get a session on a second day; **at least 50%** of comeback screens lead to a block in the same visit; and most weekly check-ins say "better than usual", with nobody reporting feeling judged. Logged as context only: install rate, bookmark rate, AI opt-in, and gut-versus-actual time per type. The last of these is the first private calibration data and is never shown as a score.

### Risks and the cut order

The biggest risk is **planner creep**: the big-one flow slowly gains fields until it recreates Marvin's first week. The guard is structural. Nothing but two optional taps comes before block one, the stamp screen carries at most two prompts, and planning lives only on the day-2 card. The second risk is that **install and push fail on iOS**, so day 2 depends on the user opening the app. The day-2 card works without push, and install rate is measured in week 3 before any further notification work. The third is **free-text privacy**. Titles and bookmarks are coursework data, and AI requests send some of it to a vendor with 30-day default retention. The guards are inventory first, opt-in per request, client-side scrubbing, and storing only accepted text. A smaller risk is **overselling evidence** whose effect sizes have shrunk. Copy promises nothing and the beta measures behaviour. If time runs out, cut in this order: the AI suggester, then wake lock, then the heads-up push (keep block-end push), then the re-spread (keep the comeback screen). Everything left after those cuts still delivers the core phase: down payment, bookmark, open terms, day-2 card and comeback screen.

## Open questions for Chris

The resolutions leave a few product calls that only Chris can make. First, **does the comeback block use the 15-minute length?** The rule says 15 is only for a big task's first block, but every proposal offered 15 on the comeback screen. The simplest reading treats the first block after a gap as a first block too. That changes one line in `firstBlockMinutes` and should go into the same ADR. Second, **what counts as a missed day** when nothing is scheduled? A workable definition: the comeback screen shows when a signed spread day passed with no block, or when two or more days passed with open terms and no block. Third, **what do open terms default to when the gut-size tap is skipped**? One option is to store no number and let the day-2 card say "Keep going on Paper?" with no time shown. Fourth, **does the due date wait until the day-2 card?** The resolutions allow only type and gut size before block one. That means a "due Friday" typed into the title is parsed, if at all, only as a suggestion. Fifth, **is "big one" right in Chris's voice**, given the voice spec (003) does not exist yet and this phase writes a lot of new copy? Sixth, **should the badge be used at all**? An `app_badge` of 1 meaning "a deal is waiting" is the only safe use, and none may be better. Seventh, **is 30-day vendor retention acceptable** for testers who opt into AI suggestions, and how should it be disclosed in the privacy inventory? Eighth, **which iPhones and iOS versions do the testers have**, and are any in the EU? Push and wake lock need iOS 18.4+ installed, and several platform facts in the notes conflict. Last, **how will "nobody reports feeling judged" be collected?** The metric needs a few short chats, which brings back the optional interviews in `docs/research/sessions/` under the usual rules: first names only, no diagnoses, not in the public repo.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "helping students with big tasks" means for Pomodeal. The obvious build is a smarter planner or an AI breakdown. The evidence and the competitor record both say the hard moments are the first minute and the morning after, and a plan helps with neither. The most defensible mechanism has a small effect: a concrete, self-named first move, worth perhaps d = 0.15 to 0.35. Pomodeal should still treat it as the product's centre, because it is cheap, robust and fully owned by the user, and the alternatives have no completion evidence at all. Open terms are the phase's quieter idea. They give Pomodeal memory without obligation: the task is remembered, but nothing is ever late.

The phase also sets a pattern for later ones. Every surface added here is judged by whether it makes the next start cheaper. Rooms, the recap and earned breaks will have to pass the same test when they return, and the friends beta's four behavioural metrics will be the first real data on whether they can.
