# Discussion Guide (45 min)

Read the **Rules** once before every session. Quotes in *italics* are what you say; the rest is for you.

## Rules

- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you use…?" every time.
- **Never say ADHD, focus disorder or productivity hack.** If they say it, don't follow it up and don't write it down.
- **Don't pitch, defend or explain.** If they're confused, that's the data. Ask *"What did you expect to happen?"*
- **Silence is a tool.** After an answer, count to 3 before your next question.
- **Follow energy.** If a story is rich, drop the next question and stay with it.
- Good probes: *"Walk me through that."* · *"What happened next?"* · *"Why do you think that is?"* · *"Can you show me?"* · *"How did that feel?"*

---

## 1. Warm-up (3 min)

*"Thanks for doing this. We're working on something for students who study a lot, and we want to understand how studying actually goes for you, not how it's supposed to go. There are no right answers. Some of it is a rough prototype at the end, and you can't hurt our feelings. Can I take notes [and record audio, just for us]? You can skip anything."*

*"What are you studying, and what does a heavy week look like right now?"*

## 2. The last study night (10 min) → Q1, Q2, Q3

*"Think about the last night you sat down to study on your own. Walk me through it, from the moment you decided to study."*

Let them tell the whole story. Then probe the moments they skipped over:

- *"How long between deciding to study and actually starting? What happened in between?"* (Q2)
- *"Where was your phone? What was open on your laptop?"* (Q3)
- *"Was there a moment you told yourself 'a few more minutes, then I'll start'?"* **Only ask this if they haven't said it already.** Note whether it was **unprompted** or **prompted**. (Q1)
- *"When you took a break, how did it end? Or did it?"*
- *"How did you feel at the end of that night?"*

## 3. What they've tried (7 min) → Q4

- *"Have you ever used anything to help you study: an app, a timer, a method, a trick?"*
- For each one: *"How long did you use it? What made you stop?"* Look for the moment it broke, like a failed day or the app making them feel bad.
- *"What do you do now instead?"*

## 4. Studying with other people (5 min) → Q5, Q8

- *"Do you ever study 'with' friends: a call, Discord, the library, a group chat?"*
- *"Tell me about the last time. What made it work or not work?"*
- *"Has a friend ever pulled you off studying? Or pulled you back to it?"*
- *"If your friends could see one thing about how your study night is going, what would be OK? What would never be OK?"* (Q8)

## 5. Prototype walkthrough (15 min) → Q6, Q7, Gate criterion 2

Hand them the phone (speed 30×). *"Please think out loud: what you see, what you expect, what's confusing. I won't help unless you're stuck for a while."*

| # | Task (say it) | Start at | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | *"Imagine it's 11 PM and you've been on Reels for a while. Use this to get yourself to start studying."* | start (Restart) | Do they find the underlined parts? Time to tap Deal? What they say about the sentence |
| T2 | *"You just opened Instagram, and this popped up. What would you do?"* | `#intercept` | Annoyed vs relieved. Then ask: *"Would you turn on something that does this? For which apps?"* (Q7) |
| T3 | *"Your Reels break is over. You want more."* | `#offer-free`, then `#offer-terms` | Do they understand borrow vs trade without help? Do they pick, or hit "No terms"? (Q6) |
| T4 | *"It's the third time you ask tonight."* | `#offer-intention` | Does naming the first thing feel helpful or like homework? |
| T5 | *"This is where your deals for the day end up. What do you make of it?"* | `#ledger`, then `#room` | Judged or informed? What they'd share with the room (Q8) |

After the tasks:

- *"What was the most useful thing you saw? The most annoying?"*
- *"Is there anything here that would make you feel bad about yourself?"* (Gate criterion 4)
- *"If a friend described this to you, what would they say it's for?"* (tests positioning without leading)

## 6. Commitment test (3 min) → Gate criterion 3

*"If you want, I can send you the link and you could try it for real the next time you study. Totally fine if not. If you do, I'll text you the day after to ask how it went."*

- Note **yes / no / maybe** and their exact words.
- Follow-up text, next day: *"Hey! Did you end up trying it last night? Either answer helps us. If you did, what happened at the end of your first break?"*

## 7. Wrap-up (2 min)

- *"Is there anything about how you study that we didn't ask about but should have?"*
- *"Who else should we talk to? Ideally someone who studies very differently from you."* (snowball recruiting for non-friends)
- Thank them and give the thank-you.
